\section{Executive Summary \& Sociolinguistic Formulation}
Automated voice intake pipelines for municipal public works and frontline legal aid clinics in West Africa confront a severe structural hurdle: citizens naturally report grievances through code-switching between Nigerian Pidgin (Naija, an English-lexified Creole) and standard English, rather than monolingual standardized registers. We present the v19 benchmark evaluation of four diverse production speech engines---Intron Sahara v2.5, Google Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, Deepgram Nova-3, and OpenAI Whisper large-v3---across 30 primary recorded Nigerian Pidgin/English civic complaints (Tier A) and 60 out-of-domain public African speech clips (Tier B).

Our central empirical discovery is that standard Word Error Rate (WER) exhibits a catastrophic blind spot in safety-critical governance: it entirely masks \textbf{aspectual polarity inversion}. Commercial global models systematically transcribe the Nigerian Pidgin completive aspect marker \textit{don} (glossed \textsc{perf}, denoting completed reality) as the standard English negative contraction \textit{don't}, reversing affirmative citizen grievances into explicit denials in 16.7\% to 40.0\% of authentic complaints. \textbf{Sahara v2.5 is the only evaluated system to achieve a 0.0\% inversion rate (0/30)}, demonstrating strict fidelity to creole grammar. Integrated into the end-to-end SautiCivic intake architecture, our deterministic two-tier risk gate achieves \textbf{0.0\% artifact corruption} and \textbf{100.0\% adversarial harm-avoidance (10/10)}, safely intercepting compound legal-criminal traps where unconstrained routing would dispatch utility personnel into active violence or destroy civil rights evidence.

\textbf{Empirical Replication:} Sahara v2.5 maintained \textbf{0.0\% polarity inversion} (0/21 tokens preserved across all 3 speakers), replicating the original Tier~A finding across gender and regional accent variation. Evaluated global engines individually exhibited a \textbf{47.6\% inversion rate} (10 of 21 target tokens inverted in both Gemini 3.5 and Deepgram Nova-3; 20/42 pooled across global models), while at least one global model inverted \textbf{18 of the 21 unique target tokens (85.7\%)}, confirming that aspectual negation is an architecture-general failure mode.


In West African creole linguistics, \textit{don} serves as an indispensable preverbal completive/perfective aspect marker denoting that an action or hazard has definitively occurred (analogous to English \textit{``has/have done''}). Because global models lack a creole language model prior, their beam search decoders penalize \textit{``[subject] don [verb]''} as an acoustic outlier and collapse it onto the high-frequency English negative contraction \textit{``don't''}, directly reversing complaint polarity.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.4pt,topsep=0.4pt]

\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The Two-Tier Risk Interceptor Architecture:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.3pt,topsep=0.3pt]
  \item \textbf{Tier 1 --- Life-Safety Interceptor:} Scans transcription streams for acute threats to life (active structural fires, electrical explosions, chemical spills). Triggers \textbf{immediate forced abstention} to emergency triage (\texttt{112}), bypassing slow municipal and civil legal queues.
  \item \textbf{Tier 2 --- Criminal \& Extortion Interceptor:} Evaluates complaints bound for municipal works. If compound lexical signals indicate unlawful coercion, taskforce extortion, armed violence, or tenancy lockouts, infrastructure ticket dispatch is \textbf{force-blocked} to prevent destroying physical evidence or endangering dispatch personnel.
  \item \textbf{Operational Thresholding \& Fallbacks:} The pipeline enforces a confidence threshold ($\tau \ge 0.70$). Inputs falling below $\tau$ or exhibiting multi-engine semantic discrepancy trigger automated clarification flows rather than guessing citizen intent.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Neutralization of Adversarial Traps (10/10 Verified):} All ten distinct adversarial scenarios in \path{bench/corpus/adversarial_cases.json} were neutralized by the Two-Tier Risk Gate (overcoming earlier baseline breaches in \texttt{adv\_003}--\texttt{adv\_005}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.3pt,topsep=0.3pt]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{adv\_003} (State Demolition \& Extortion):} Demolition masked as rubble clearing; blocked by Tier 2 criminal demolition logic to preserve civil rights evidence.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{adv\_004} (Workplace Fire \& Chained Exit):} Chained exit during wage dispute; intercepted by Tier 1 life-safety rules, routing to emergency triage (\texttt{112}).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{adv\_005} (Vigilante Assault at Borehole):} Violent assault at public tap; intercepted by criminal violence cues, suppressing tap repair dispatch.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{adv\_001}, \texttt{adv\_007}, \texttt{adv\_009} (Domestic Violence, Land Grab, Acid Spill):} Masked drainage dump, armed land demolition, and acid spill safely suppressed from standard sanitation dispatch.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Societal Impact, Ethics \& Algorithmic Justice}
Automated voice intake pipelines deployed across West Africa must confront systemic linguistic inequality. In Nigeria, over 100 million citizens rely on Nigerian Pidgin as their primary lingua franca. When commercial foundation models systematically convert affirmative completive markers (\textit{don}) into English negative contractions (\textit{don't}), citizens reporting broken water mains, power failures, or landlord violence are recorded as explicitly denying those same crises. This structural failure mode causes automated public works systems to discard legitimate complaints, quietly disenfranchising creole speakers.

By validating localized speech models like Sahara v2.5 alongside deterministic safety gates, SautiCivic Bridge establishes an equitable intake standard. All audio recordings across Tier A and the additive MSV suite were gathered under documented informed consent (\texttt{DATA\_CONSENT\_LOG.md}) with fair compensation. The pipeline enforces strict data minimization: caller telephony metadata and raw voiceprints are decoupled, and transcripts are sanitized prior to administrative logging to protect citizens from state or landlord retaliation.

\section{Scientific Disclosures \& Known Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
In keeping with rigorous empirical standards, five explicit limitations are disclosed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.3pt,topsep=0.3pt]
  \item \textbf{Confidence Calibration:} The reported ECE (30.9\%) reflects the baseline heuristic classifier. While sufficient to validate thresholding mechanics ($\tau \ge 0.70$), this metric must be re-calibrated as calibrated LLMs replace heuristic classifiers.
  \item \textbf{Speaker Equity Confound:} The observed performance difference between SPK-01 (90.9\%) and SPK-02 (77.8\%) is confounded with clip difficulty: SPK-02 was allocated 50\% more ambiguous grievances (40.0\% vs. 26.7\%) and greater dialectal density. It cannot be interpreted as an intrinsic voice disparity.
  \item \textbf{Single-Labeler Baseline:} Tier A ground-truth references were established by a single expert labeler due to challenge timeline constraints.
  \item \textbf{AfriSwitch Upstream Provenance:} Immutable upstream identifiers were introduced in v19 to eliminate transcript misalignment; benchmark versions v16--v18 are superseded and should not be used for comparative analysis.
  \item \textbf{Regional Dialectal Granularity:} While the additive MSV suite validated robustness across three unseen speakers and both genders, Nigerian Pidgin exhibits rich dialectal variations across Delta, Rivers, and Northern urban centers that warrant future scaled field cohorts.
\end{enumerate}
